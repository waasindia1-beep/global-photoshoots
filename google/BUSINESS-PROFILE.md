## 2. Storefront or service-area business: get this right first

This is the first decision, most people get it wrong, and getting it wrong can lead to suspension.

**Storefront business.** The street address is publicly shown on the profile, on Google Maps and in the knowledge panel. It is a stronger local ranking signal because proximity to the searcher feeds into ranking. You need a genuine, customer-facing place of business. Google can and does verify by visiting, by satellite and street view imagery, and by asking for a video of the premises.

**Service-area business.** The street address is not shown to customers. You define the areas you cover instead. No storefront, no customer walk-ins required, no signage needed. This is designed exactly for a business that works from home and travels or works remotely.

**Recommendation: service-area business.**

You work from 5B/23, Vishnu Garden, you have no walk-in studio, and no customer will ever stand on your doorstep. Listing that as a storefront creates three separate problems:

1. Google requires a residential address to have a genuine business use. If a customer, a competitor or a Google reviewer reports that no business operates there, Google can suspend the profile and the reviews go with it.
2. Verification gets harder, not easier. Postcards and video verification are both harder when there is no sign, no staff and no walk-in door. You may end up needing a utility bill or a business licence you do not have.
3. It looks wrong. A Maps pin inside a residential lane for a photography studio reads as untrustworthy to a brand owner who is comparing you with Lohar Studio.

Service-area gives up the proximity signal, but you get one clean profile, easy verification and no suspension risk. For a seller who is chosen on price, turnaround and accuracy rather than on who is nearest, the address is not what wins the order.

**The one condition that changes this answer.** If you genuinely can receive customers at 5B/23 Vishnu Garden, and there is a real, visitable place of business there with your name on it, choose storefront. That means a dedicated work area a customer can be let into, stock and equipment visible, and a way to receive business mail. If customers arrive and the door opens, use storefront. If they never arrive and mail is a neighbour's problem, use service-area.

**Never use** a PO box, a virtual office, a coworking space, or an address where no part of the work happens. These are the addresses that get profiles suspended and are the second most common reason a new profile is rejected.

## 5. Verification

Google verifies that a business exists at the location and is reachable at the phone number. You will not appear in Maps until it passes.

**Ask for video verification first.** On a service-area profile, and on any profile where the address is residential, Google usually offers video over postcard. Request it from the dashboard under Get verified.

How video verification works:

- You get a link, you record a short video on a phone, you upload it from the dashboard.
- Show the front of the building or the gate, the street name or number plate, and something that shows the work: your kit, a lighting setup, finished images on a screen, a product box with your order details.
- You do not need to speak. Some requests ask you to read a phrase on screen.
- Keep the video under three minutes, no editing, one continuous shot.
- Decision time is typically three to five business days.

**Postcard.** Google posts a card with a five-digit code. Allow three to fourteen days; allow the full fourteen before you assume it is lost. India is served; international addresses are not. Enter the code in the dashboard. If the card arrives damaged or without a legible code, request a replacement from the dashboard rather than re-verifying from scratch.

**If it is rejected.**

The single most common reason new profiles get rejected is a name that is not the real-world business name. A profile called "Best Product Photography Delhi 24 Hour 999" fails verification often enough that it is the first thing to change. Strip the name to `Global Photoshoots` and re-submit.

In order of likelihood, the other reasons are:

1. Address cannot be verified, usually a residential address with no sign, or a virtual office, a PO box or a coworking space.
2. Phone number not reachable. Check the WhatsApp number is switched on and can take a call, not just a message.
3. The primary category has no supporting evidence in the photos.
4. A virtual office, a mail drop or a friend's address.

What to do, in order:

1. Read the rejection notice word for word. It usually names the field.
2. Fix that one field. Change nothing else, because a second failure is harder to appeal than a first.
3. Re-submit a video that shows the door plate, the street, and the kit or the finished images.
4. If the address is the problem and you have no alternative, use the Contact us form in the verification panel and explain it is a home-based service-area business with no walk-in location. Be consistent in the story across Google, Justdial, IndiaMART and the website.
5. Do not create a second profile while the first is pending. Two profiles for one business is a suspension risk and splits your reviews.

Do not use a mail-forwarding service, a friend's address or a commercial address where no work happens. A suspended profile loses the reviews, and rebuilding from zero with Lohar Studio at 638+ reviews is a much worse position than waiting two weeks for a code.

## 6. Photos: the first nine

This is the part most people get wrong, and the part Google weighs most heavily for a photography business. A photographer with five grey photos ranks badly and is not called.

**Technical minimums for every upload:** sRGB, JPEG or PNG, under 10 MB each. Marketplace images go up at 2000 x 2000 pixels, 1:1, which is also the Amazon and Myntra spec. Process and behind-the-scenes images go up at 1500 x 1000 pixels, 4:3 or 16:10, because they crop well in the feed. Google does not mandate a ratio for content photos, but anything below 1200 pixels wide looks broken on a desktop browser.

### 1. Profile photo / logo

- `720 x 720` pixels, 1:1, PNG.
- A clean wordmark: `GLOBAL PHOTOSHOOTS` in white on a near-black background, with the name wide and centred so it survives Google's circular crop.
- Also prepare a headshot version of yourself at the same size, holding one finished image. It is allowed as the profile photo and it converts better than a logo, because buyers are buying a person.
- This appears on Maps and in the knowledge panel. It is not a ranking factor. Spend twenty minutes on it, not two hours.

### 2. Cover image

- `1200 x 750` pixels, 8:5. Google asks for at least 750 x 500 and crops to a wide banner on desktop and to near-square on mobile.
- Keep the subject in the middle third with empty space either side, or mobile users see a cropped-out product.
- Content: one finished product scene, warm and clean, with the wordmark small in one corner and no price text.
- This is the second image people see and it does the persuading. Make it the best image you have.

### 3 to 9. The seven content photos

Post these as individual posts, each with a caption, not as one album. Individual posts get more surface area.

1. `Marketplace main image, white background` - 2000 x 2000. A single product on pure white with correct margins. Caption explains this is the spec Amazon and Flipkart require.
2. `Lifestyle scene` - 2000 x 2000. The same product in a real setting, built around the client's file. Strongest image for a D2C audience.
3. `Before and after, side by side` - 1600 x 1000. Left: the client's original product photo. Right: the finished scene. This single image answers the objection every brand owner has, which is whether you are inventing their product. You can show the input and the output, which proves you did not.
4. `How a scene is built` - 1500 x 1000. A flat-lay of the lighting, background and reference material behind one output.
5. `A video clip, four frames` - 1500 x 1000. Four stills from one clip in a strip, to show motion work without uploading video.
6. `Detail shot` - 1500 x 1000. Packaging, texture or a label close-up. Proves the product accuracy that you sell on.
7. `Price panel` - 1500 x 1000. A plain slate with the pack prices written out: Trial ₹499, Starter ₹2,499, Growth ₹6,999, Scale ₹17,999, overflow from ₹120. The ₹499 and ₹2,499 anchors do the work.

### You have no client work yet: build a demo set and label it honestly

Buy four to six cheap physical products yourself, or use your own. A water bottle, a tin, a mug, a skincare box, a watch strap, a small lamp, anything with a label and a colour that is easy to get wrong. Then:

- Photograph each one yourself, in ordinary light, on your phone. Keep those input files.
- Produce the full output set from those inputs using your process.
- Shoot the finished images for the profile.
- Label every one of them: `Demo set. Product and scene created by Global Photoshoots. Not client work.`

Six to ten honest demo images are enough to launch. What you are not going to do is post Lohar Studio's work, Ckstudio's work, or a product image you found on a brand's Instagram as your own. That is a misrepresentation, it is discoverable in one reverse image search, and a single report from a competitor or a client can take the profile down. It is also the exact thing that gets people sued.

Keep the raw input photos in the same folder and keep publishing them. The before-and-after post is your proof of honesty and it is also your most persuasive asset.

Set a diary date for 30 days after launch. On that date, replace the demo images with real client work as it arrives, and mark each one `Client work`.

Once the profile is live, add one new photo to the library every week and one new post every week. Freshness is the only ranking lever you fully control.

## 7. Reviews: from zero to your first ten

You have no reviews. Lohar Studio has 638+ and 4.9 stars. The gap is not closed by anything you post, it is closed one client at a time.

**The rule, stated plainly.** Google prohibits incentivised reviews and review gating. Never offer a discount, a free extra image, a cash note, a gift or a free pack in exchange for a review, and never offer anything in exchange for a five-star rating. Never write a review yourself or on a client's behalf. Never trade reviews with another business. Offer nothing at all, and say so in the message so the client does not assume there is a deal attached.

**How to ask without looking desperate.** Ask once, briefly, in a message that already asks for something else, and accept the answer. Never ask for a specific star rating, never ask twice, never ask on the day of handover, and never send a template that says "please leave us a 5-star review".

A profile that posts ranks better than one that does not. There is no single lever you control as cleanly. Two posts a week, one photo a week, replies inside 48 hours.

## 12. Honesty rules to keep in every field

The same line has to hold everywhere, and you should expect to say it out loud on a call.

- You do not physically shoot. Never say "we shot your product", "our studio shoot", "on our set" or "our photographer was there".
- You build the scene, lighting and background around the client's real product photo. Say exactly that.
- Never describe output as a photograph you took. "Image" and "scene" are accurate. "Our photograph" is not.
- Never post anyone else's work as yours, including as a "mood board" or "reference", unless the post says in the first line whose work it is.
- If someone asks directly "are these photographs you took", the answer is: no, this is built from your product photo, and here is the input file next to the output so you can see the product has not changed.

Say that in the description, in your WhatsApp reply, in the Q&A box and in the demo photo captions, and the profile becomes a credibility asset rather than a liability.

## 13. What not to do

- No PO box, no virtual office, no coworking space, no address where no work happens.
- No residential address as a storefront unless customers genuinely come there.
- No keyword stuffing in the name. No prices, no "best", no phone numbers, no "24 hour".
- No second profile for the same business.
- No incentivised reviews, no review gating, no five-star requests, no reviews written for clients, no review trading.
- No competitor's images, no client's images without written permission, no images lifted from a brand's Instagram.
- No hours you do not work.
- No paid Justdial or Sulekha tier in the first three months.
- No second website, no link shorteners, no Instagram in the website field. Use the Vercel URL with `?src=gbp`.
