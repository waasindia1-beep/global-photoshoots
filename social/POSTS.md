# Twelve posts, and why they are teaching posts

Global Photoshoots, New Delhi. Product photography and short product video, Rs 249 to Rs 1299 a project, delivery in 24 to 48 hours, every file belongs to the client.

This is the posting plan. Twelve posts, fully written, ready to paste. It is built around one fact: six posts, seven followers, and no finished client work yet.

## 1. The position, said plainly

You have no finished portfolio work. No reviews, no testimonials, no case studies, nothing to show a stranger that proves you have done this a hundred times.

So the grid is not a portfolio. The moment you treat it as one, it looks empty and you start inventing things to fill it. Do not do that.

The other route is legitimate, and it is the route the trade itself is moving towards: post proof of capability and teach the work.

**Proof of capability** means showing the method. The set, the lighting, the background, the spec checks, the handover. A buyer who lands on five posts that each show you understanding one specific thing will trust you with a project. A buyer who lands on nine finished frames and no explanation has no way to tell who built them.

**Education** means writing the answer to the question a seller is already asking at midnight. Why is my main image rejected. Why does my product look different in the photo. How do I know the set will be right before I pay for forty images. Those answers get saved, shared and messaged, and they are the exact questions that make a stranger open WhatsApp.

This is a strategy, not a workaround. Never describe it as one, and never apologise for the account size anywhere.

### What stalling costs, in numbers

| Studio | Position | Result |
|---|---|---|
| One studio with a full SEO website, built properly | 0 posts, 5 followers | Invisible. The website did not carry it. |
| One studio whose Instagram handle is linked from its own live website | The handle now resolves to a dead page | The one link a buyer trusts is broken. |

Both did the obvious thing. They waited for work to arrive. An empty grid is not neutral, it is a negative signal, because a buyer reads it as this person is not trading.

### How to make it look deliberate

| Do this | Not this |
|---|---|
| Open the caption with the service, the city and the price | Open with Hi guys, New post, or an apology for the grid |
| Teach one specific thing per post | Post a finished frame with no reason behind it |
| Say what you do in the first two lines: set, lighting and background built around the client's real product photos | Let a buyer work out whether you even hold a camera |
| Use real product photos, yours or a client's with permission, and show the before | Post a frame with a product nobody can identify |
| Publish on the days in section 5 and keep them | Post three times in one day, then nothing for a month |
| Ask for the order in the caption | Say DM for details |
| Turn the first question into the next post | Delete a post because the answer is uncomfortable |
| Never write a client name, testimonial or review count you do not have | Invent a case study to fill the grid |

Two more things that make it read as deliberate. Post every week for eight weeks without a gap, even in a week where the only post is a screen recording of a build. And pin your three strongest posts in the order set out in SETUP.md, so a new visitor reads method, then price, then proof.

### Post 8. Carousel. The five mistakes that get an Amazon main image rejected

**Slides, in order**

1. **Cover.** Five mistakes that get an Amazon main image rejected. Small line at the bottom: Product photography New Delhi, from Rs 249.
2. **Mistake one, the product does not fill the frame.** Amazon wants the product to fill roughly 85% of the image. Too small and the listing looks amateur, too large and it touches the edge and gets pulled.
3. **Mistake two, the background is not white.** Off white, cream, light grey and gradients all get flagged. If you are lighting on a white sheet, the grey shadow underneath is what usually fails it.
4. **Mistake three, text, props or extra logos.** No text, no watermark, no price tag, no brand logos other than the product's own, no hands, no plants, no packaging that is not in the box.
5. **Mistake four, colour that does not match the real product.** This one gets done by accident. If the image is warmer, cooler or more saturated than the item the buyer receives, the reviews follow the order. Leave the product layer alone.
6. **Mistake five, wrong size or format.** Amazon wants a minimum of 1600 px on the longest side, RGB, JPEG or PNG, inside the file size limit. A phone photo exported wrong fails an otherwise perfect listing.
7. **The fix in one line.** Photograph the product straight on, one light, no shadow underneath, accurate colour, then build the scene around it, never through it.

### Rules for the schedule

- Never skip two weeks in a row. A gap reads as a closed shop, and reopening costs you more than the week you saved.
- Never post the same finished frame twice. Change the product, change the hook, change the first line.
- Reuse the caption body when the answer is the same, because it already carries price, marketplaces, city and the number. Change the opening three lines.
- Answer every comment on day one within the hour. Reply speed is the cheapest reach there is at this account size.
- Turn every question that arrives on WhatsApp into a post within the week. That is the whole content engine, and it costs nothing.
- After four weeks, check Accounts discovered under Professional dashboard. That is the number that says whether any of this is working. Saves, DMs and one repeat order tell you the rest.
